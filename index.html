Full-Pitch Tracking of a Thrust-Vectoring Aerial Manipulator: A Simulation Study of Equivalent Coupling Effects

Baoju Zhang, Yongran Zhi, Lei Liu, Huijin Fan, and Bo Wang

研究概览

本文通过仿真研究带有四个可倾转推进单元和五自由度机械臂的空中操作平台，考察预设质心与惯量变化下的完整俯仰旋转跟踪。研究结合四元数姿态反馈、扩张状态观测器和非线性控制分配，并比较两种参考信号生成方式。结果提供了初步数值证据，同时指出完成延迟和位置漂移等局限。

Abstract / 英文摘要

Near-wall manipulation motivates aerial platforms that accommodate arm reach without bringing the airframe unnecessarily close to a surface. This paper studies a thrustvectoring aerial manipulator with four tiltable propulsion units and a five-degree-of-freedom arm, focusing on full-pitch tracking under prescribed mass-property variations. Thrust reorientation provides a design basis for vertical-surface operations, although workspace and aerodynamic benefits are not evaluated here. Near a 90◦ pitch attitude, differential-thrust pitch authority diminishes, while nonlinear thrust–moment allocation and changing mass distribution complicate tracking. The control architecture combines quaternion attitude feedback, filtered proportional– integral–derivative rate control, and fixed-gain extended state observers for equivalent-disturbance estimation and compensation. A nonlinear allocator coordinates rotor thrusts and differential tilt to target three moments and a collective-lift projection. Conditional continuous-time analysis establishes a finite-time bound for an auxiliary local pitch model under uniform residual bounds, a suitable Lyapunov family, and an invariant-domain condition; its correspondence with the two-branch digital controller is not established. Paired simulations evaluate a 0.2 rad /s pitch revolution with a 3-cm forward and 2-cm body-down center-ofmass shift and a 10% inertia increase. Under complete-reference replay and online reference generation, perturbed peak geodesic errors of the simulated attitude estimates over 20–65 s are 4.833◦ and 4.886◦, respectively, while position-error peaks are 2.406 m and 0.2913 m. Both perturbed cases maintain peak attitudeestimate tracking errors below 5◦, and their recorded attitude estimates subsequently traverse the complete revolution. With online reference generation, the position-error peak increases by only 0.0080 m relative to the nominal case; replay instead exhibits substantial lateral drift. These results provide encouraging preliminary numerical evidence of attitude-estimate tracking under the prescribed mass-property variations and highlight the role of reference feedback in position regulation. Completion delay and configuration-dependent position drift remain limitations.
